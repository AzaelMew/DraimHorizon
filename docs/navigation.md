# Experimental navigation

Open **J → Experiments → Baritone** and accept the experimental warning if prompted. Choose **Navigation**, **Mining**, **Tunnel**, **Bridge**, **Farm**, **Drain** or **Schematics**. [Tunnel and bridge controls](construction.md).

1. Enter **X**, **Y** and **Z** in three separate fields (pasting `x y z` fills all three), click **Waypoint** to cycle through saved destinations in your current dimension, or click **Player** to choose a nearby loaded player. The selectors are mutually exclusive and return to coordinate input after the last entry. Editing a coordinate clears both selections.
2. There are no action buttons. Assign the four **Baritone** keys (preview, start/resume, pause, stop) in **Options → Mods → DraimHorizon**; they work from every Baritone tab. See [automation](automation.md#global-automation-keys).
3. Preview draws a route without moving. Start calculates a route and starts walking. Movement keys, jump, sneak or opening a screen pause navigation; Start continues with a fresh route and Stop clears the destination.

With a player selected, **Go** becomes **Follow**. The navigator tracks that player's UUID without requiring the relay, maintains a short distance and recalculates as they move. It stops moving immediately if the player is no longer loaded nearby. It waits up to 10 seconds for them to reappear; after that, press the start key once they are visible again. Manual pause always requires the start key and never restarts on its own.

The line shows the remaining planned segment. The HUD shows status and straight-line distance to the destination. Long routes are calculated in segments as real chunks become available. The camera remains under your control.

The walker (shared by every Baritone task: walking, following, mining, tunnel, bridge, farm, drain and schematics) steps to the four sides, up one block or down up to three, and also across corners when both sides of the corner are open, so open ground is crossed in straight diagonal lines. Partial blocks are judged by their real shape: anything up to 0.6 high (a bottom slab, a carpet, a bed, a snow layer, a stonecutter) is stepped over without a jump; a shape about a block high that covers nearly the whole cell (stairs, a top slab, a chest, a composter, soul sand, a cauldron) is a block to stand on; fences, walls, panes, bars and fence gates are a block and a half high and are never stood on or walked through. Wooden doors and fence gates on the route are opened by hand when the walker reaches them and closed again behind it (iron doors are not opened). Wooden trapdoors are walked over when shut on the floor and opened when they are a hatch at head height or in the way of a ladder; iron doors and trapdoors are not opened. Ladders and vines are climbed up and down. Drops of up to three blocks are stepped off as a matter of course; when nothing else leads down (a roof, a cliff) a longer drop is taken if its fall damage is at most three health points and leaves more than ten: six blocks without protection, eight with Feather Falling IV boots, up to twelve with full fall protection (Feather Falling and Protection on the armour). Stairs or a slope nearby are always preferred. A goal more than 24 blocks away or at another height is planned with a wide search over 96 blocks around, so a far way down (a slope at the other end of a second floor) is found in one go; far walks are taken in legs of up to 72 blocks and never go back and forth. Every walk that arrives is remembered for the world, and the next walk between the same places follows it instead of searching again. The walker moves like a person: it looks a few blocks ahead along its route and turns its head smoothly, takes corners in a curve, turns round rather than walking backwards, keeps walking for a moment while the next part of the route is planned, and turns to a block it aims at instead of snapping to it. This rule, like the shapes, doors, trapdoors, ladders and diagonal steps, lives in the one shared planner, so every task that walks follows it. When a far walk is taken in legs, each leg ends on the ground nearest to the height the walk is heading for, so a walk back to a farm ends at the farm's level and not on a roof or a tree above it. It avoids fluids and hazardous blocks; unavailable chunks are never assumed to be empty. Changed blocks or a stalled character trigger replanning, with at most three recovery attempts near one location before stopping. Planning waits for the player to land after a jump. When no walkable route exists, or the character stalls, the navigator plans again with permission to **break** blocks in the way and to **place** a block under a gap (this needs a suitable tool and building blocks in the hotbar, inventory or off hand). Each new route is calculated from the current position; after a few failed attempts it stops with a reason. Vertical pillaring is not supported.

Navigation resets after disconnecting, changing dimensions/worlds/accounts or disabling the mod. It never starts automatically on joining. It does not use Seed Map predictions for movement.

## Ore mining

In **Baritone → Mining**, toggle what to collect in the grid of item icons (hover for the name), like the structure filters on the map; several can be selected and the nearest of any of them is mined. Page through the grid with the **Selected … →** button. There is no block limit: press the start key to begin and the stop key to end. Besides the ten highlighted ores the list has stone, cobblestone, dirt, sand, gravel, clay, wood, obsidian, glowstone, nether stone, sandstone, end stone, pumpkins and melons. The preview key searches and displays a route without attacking. Carry a suitable tool anywhere in the hotbar or inventory (pickaxe for ore and stone, axe for wood and pumpkins, shovel for dirt, sand and gravel); the right one is put in hand for each block. Leave a free inventory slot and press the start key. The camera turns toward each target when breaking it; manual camera adjustment, attack/use input, movement or an open screen pauses the task.

The miner uses the same ore table as the **Ores** experiment: ore marks it shows are tried first, then loaded chunks are scanned (radius 24). Buried ore is reached by digging a route to it. Seed Map predictions are not used. Blocks beneath the player's support area, ore beside fluids/unknown terrain, and ore under falling blocks are excluded. It stops when no accessible ore remains, the tool is unsuitable or nearly broken, the inventory needs a free slot, or the server refuses or times out a break.

The counter measures server-confirmed ore blocks. After each block the miner walks to the items it dropped and waits for the pickup before taking the next block, which is always the nearest one, so veins and whole trunks are worked through. A trunk inside its crown counts as reachable (the leaves in the way are cut first), and a wide trunk (a 2x2 spruce) is also cut from inside, standing under the logs. When the rest of a tall trunk hangs more than five blocks over the ground and nothing to stand on reaches it, the miner builds a column of earth or stone under itself (jump, put a block where the feet were) until it stands four blocks under the lowest log, cuts what it reaches from there, and then digs its column away from the top, back to the ground. It needs blocks enough for the column (dirt, netherrack or cobblestone first; never the kind being mined). Pause/Resume preserves progress within the current session; disconnecting or changing worlds resets the task.

This is an independent native Rust navigator. It does not load the Java Baritone library. Straight tunnels and floor placement are described in the construction guide; [Farming and schematic building](automation.md) have their own controls and limits. Flight automation is not implemented.
